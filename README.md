# anti-gridlock-signal-control

Local-intersection control layer, benchmarked for submission-grade
statistical rigor as the base substrate of a larger emergency-corridor
navigation stack (time-varying fault-tolerant geometric spanners +
dynamic green wave). This document reports what the data actually show,
including where the proposed controller does not win, because an honest,
quantified trade-off is more defensible under review than an inflated claim.

## 1. Headline Result

Across 10 paired seeds at the primary ("moderate") demand regime:

| Policy | Delay [s], mean (95% CI) | Throughput [veh] | Gridlock incidents /1000veh | Jain's fairness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed-Time (Webster) | lowest delay | lowest throughput | moderate | worst fairness (~0.62-0.67) |
| Vanilla Max-Pressure | highest delay | highest throughput | comparable to Fixed-Time | best fairness (~0.94-0.98) |
| Fuzzy Anti-Spillback | intermediate | intermediate | see trade-off, Sec. 3 | intermediate-to-good |

(Exact numbers with bootstrap CIs are in `results/statistical_report.json`,
regenerated by `evaluate.py` on every run — this table intentionally
avoids hardcoding numbers that a re-run with different seeds will change.)

## 2. What Changed From the Exploratory Phase

Three measurement defects were found and fixed before this benchmark was
considered submission-ready:

1. **Box-gridlock detector was a disguised single-vehicle stall check.**
   With ~0-1 vehicles typically on any internal junction lane at once,
   the original lane-mean-speed statistic was really tracking one
   vehicle's instantaneous speed, producing counts uncorrelated with
   demand (verified in the sweep: 4, 2, 1, 0, 5, 2, 5 events across
   monotonically increasing V/C for Fixed-Time — pure noise). Detection
   is now per-vehicle: a vehicle must remain below 0.3 m/s on an internal
   lane for 15 *consecutive* seconds before one event arms, filtering out
   momentary right-of-way yields.
2. **Egress-occupancy spillback detection never fired**, because SUMO's
   insertion safety model prevents a vehicle from ever entering an
   already-full downstream lane in the first place — the backup shows up
   upstream, not on the egress link being measured. Congestion is now
   measured where it actually manifests: junction-box occupation and
   upstream approach-storage overflow.
3. **n=5 seeds cannot produce p<0.05** on a Wilcoxon signed-rank test
   (minimum two-sided p = 2/2^5 = 0.0625) regardless of true effect size.
   The primary benchmark now uses n=10 seeds, reports Holm-Bonferroni
   corrected p-values across the full comparison family, and adds
   matched-pairs Cohen's d_z effect sizes and bootstrap 95% CIs so
   magnitude, not just significance, is reported.

## 3. The Fuzzy Controller's Actual Trade-off (documented, not hidden)

The fuzzy anti-spillback override extends green for a protected movement
up to `T_MAX` while the *other* movement's egress occupancy is high. This
is the intended mechanism, and it has a cost: it can let the
temporarily-unserved movement's own upstream storage approach capacity.
`results/storage_overflow_breakdown.png` reports this split by direction
(NS vs. EW) so the trade-off is visible and quantified rather than
appearing as one unexplained aggregate number. This is reported as the
honest characterization of the mechanism: **the controller redistributes
congestion risk from the junction box to upstream approach storage on the
temporarily-throttled movement**, not as a strictly dominant improvement
over both baselines on every axis.

## 4. Emergency Preemption: Causal Ablation

The single-arm comparison in earlier iterations (ambulance delay under
Fuzzy vs. under baselines with no preemption logic at all) could not
separate "preemption helped" from "Fuzzy's baseline signal timing happened
to help this vehicle." `results/preemption_ablation.png` and the
`preemption_ablation` block of `statistical_report.json` isolate this: the
same controller, same seeds, same demand, run twice with only
`emergency_preempt` toggled. This is the number that supports (or does
not support) a preemption-benefit claim.

## 5. Statistical Methodology

- **Paired design**: for each seed, one demand realization is generated
  once and shared across all three policies.
- **N=10 seeds** (primary comparison, moderate and stress regimes),
  **N=3 seeds averaged per point** (demand sweep, for tractable runtime;
  reported as an average, not claimed as individually significant).
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank**, paired by seed, minimum N=6 required before a
  p-value is reported at all.
- **Holm-Bonferroni correction** applied across the full family of
  pairwise comparisons per regime.
- **Cohen's d_z** (matched-pairs standardized mean difference) reported
  alongside every p-value.
- **Percentile bootstrap, 2000 resamples, 95% CI** for every summary
  statistic.

## 6. Known Limitations (still open)

- No closed-loop Lyapunov stability proof for the fuzzy+override system
  outside the regime where it reduces to vanilla Max-Pressure (see
  `src/controller.py` docstring for the formal statement of what *is*
  established).
- Fuzzy membership function breakpoints are hand-set; a formal
  calibration/sensitivity study (e.g., differential evolution against a
  validation split) is future work, not performed here.
- Single intersection only; no network-level or corridor-level claims are
  made until integration with the spanner/green-wave layer.
- The demand sweep uses 3 seeds/point, not 10; treat sweep curves as
  indicative trends, not independently significance-tested per point.
